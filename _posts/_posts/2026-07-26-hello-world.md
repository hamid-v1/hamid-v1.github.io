## Hey, I'm Hamid

Welcome to my corner of the internet. This is where I'll be documenting CTF writeups, offensive tooling I build, HTB lab breakdowns, and general notes from my time in the security world. Before diving into the technical stuff, I figured it's worth introducing myself properly.

## Where I'm Coming From

I've spent the past year deep in **reverse engineering**, working through low-level binaries and learning how software actually behaves once you strip away the abstractions. There's something satisfying about disassembling a binary and slowly piecing together the logic the compiler tried to bury — that process is what pulled me deeper into offensive security in the first place.

Alongside that, I've been competing solo in international CTF events, including **Blackhat CTF** and **SekaiCTF**, and I stay active on [ctftime.org](https://ctftime.org), picking up challenges wherever I can. Competing solo means carrying every category yourself — web, crypto, pwn, rev — which has forced me to get comfortable being a generalist while still going deep on the areas I care about most, like binary exploitation and reverse engineering.

## Hack The Box: More Than Just Solving Boxes

A big part of my practical learning has come from **Hack The Box**. What keeps me coming back isn't just finishing a box — it's the process of **vulnerability chaining**: finding one small misconfiguration or clue that quietly opens the door to the next, and the next, until a seemingly hardened system falls apart step by step. That kind of chained thinking is something you can't really learn from theory alone. It's a skill built entirely through hands-on practice, and I think it's one of the most valuable things HTB teaches — how real attackers actually think, not just how individual vulnerabilities work in isolation.

## Building Offensive Tools — In Rust

When I'm not solving challenges, I like building software — specifically, tools that solve real security problems rather than just proof-of-concept scripts. My language of choice for this is **Rust**. I'm drawn to it for offensive security tooling because of the balance it strikes: memory safety without sacrificing the low-level control you need when working close to the system, plus the performance to make tools genuinely usable in real engagements rather than just toy projects.

My goal with these tools isn't just personal practice — I want to build things that help companies **strengthen their own defenses** by understanding exactly how they'd be attacked. Offense and defense aren't opposites; the best defensive thinking comes from understanding offense deeply, and that's the philosophy I try to build around.

## Beyond the Screen

Technical skill aside, I genuinely enjoy the **community side** of this field. I like meeting people who share these interests, hearing how they got started, what pulled them into security, and what mistakes or breakthroughs shaped their path. Everyone's route into this field looks different, and those conversations are often as valuable as any writeup or exploit.
